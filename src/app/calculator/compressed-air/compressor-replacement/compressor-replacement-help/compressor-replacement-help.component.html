Zero-Flow Power
Total package input power at zero flow / fully unloaded. Left uncorrected by the pressure adjustment, since unloaded power is far less pressure-sensitive.

Enter 0 for a unit that draws no power when unloaded; that is a valid value and the performance profile will still plot. For start/stop control it must be 0. It must always be less than full-load power.
} @if (currentField == 'averagePower') {
Average Power at Operating Pressure
A single measured or estimated average package power, used in place of the full-load/zero-flow blend. Enter it as the power drawn at the operating pressure shown above — it already reflects that pressure, so it is used exactly as entered and is not corrected again.

On the Modification side, Alter Operations For lets this row derive its power from a baseline compressor instead. The baseline reading is then re-scaled by the change in operating pressure:
kWmod = kWbase × [((Pmod+Palt,mod)/Palt,mod)0.283 − 1] ÷ [((Pbase+Palt,base)/Palt,base)0.283 − 1]

Because both sides share the same rated-pressure term, it cancels — no nameplate rated pressure is needed for this mode. Equal operating pressures give exactly the baseline power.

} @if (currentField == 'operatingPressure') {
Operating Pressure
Discharge pressure to evaluate the compressor at (Pdischarge). Leave it equal to the rated pressure for no correction; lower it to model running at reduced pressure.

Full-load and ladder power are scaled by the pressure-inlet correction:
kWadj = kWrated × [((Pdischarge+Palt)/Palt)0.283 − 1] ÷ [((Prated+Psea)/Psea)0.283 − 1], with exponent (1.395−1)/1.395.

The denominator is referenced to sea level (Psea = 14.7 psia / 101.325 kPaa) because rated data is published at sea level. At altitude, reaching the same gauge pressure requires more work, so the factor rises above 1 and the profile curve can sit above 100% even at rated pressure.

Source: NREL Uniform Methods Project, Chapter 22 — Compressed Air Evaluation Protocol, p. 7, Eq. 3 (research-hub.nlr.gov/en/publications/chapter-22-compressed-air-evaluation-protocol-the-uniform-methods).
