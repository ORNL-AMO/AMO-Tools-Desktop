Nameplate Data
Enter general information that can be found on the motor's nameplate.
Efficiency Class
Efficiency Class is the classification of efficiency given to motor. This can be found either on the nameplate or based on how the motor compares with the NEMA MG 1-2003.
Efficiency Class options include Standard Efficiency, Energy Efficient, or Premium Efficient

The Premium (IE3) and Energy Efficient (IE2) classes are based on motors that match those NEMA classes. Standard motors should be used if the efficiency is below Energy Efficient (IE2). Efficiency values can also be directly input using User Specified.
Estimated Nominal Efficiency
Efficiency at full load. This can often be found on the nameplate but can also be estimated by MEASUR
"Estimate Efficiency" will predict the motor's nominal efficiency. The predicted efficiency is based on models generated by PSAT 2008 using TEFC (Totally Enclosed, Fan-Cooled) motor data from MotorMaster. The models use the following predictor variables:
  • Efficiency Class
  • Full-Load Speed
  • Rated Motor Power
  • Line Frequency
Note: If the values used to predict the efficiency are changed then the button will have to be re-clicked to update the predicted Efficiency.
Note: This calculation is only valid for 3-phase AC motors

Line Frequency
The frequency level of the AC electricity used in transmission and distribution from electricity generation sources to customers (utility frequency, power line frequency, mains frequency). In North or South America and parts of Asia, this is usually 60 Hz. In many other countries, it is 50 Hz

Motor Supply
The type of electrical supply the motor runs on. Options are AC Three-Phase, AC Single-Phase, or DC. Motors added before this option existed are treated as AC Three-Phase.
Electrical input power for each type is:
  • AC Three-Phase: √3 × V × I × PF
  • AC Single-Phase: V × I × PF
  • DC: V × I
Energy use in the inventory comes from rated power, load factor, operating hours, utilization factor and efficiency, so it is calculated the same way for all three types.

Note: The Estimate and Calculate options in the motor catalog are based on three-phase induction motor data, so they are turned off for single-phase and DC motors. Use values from the nameplate or a field measurement instead.
Note: For reference, minimum efficiencies for single-phase capacitor-start motors from 0.25 to 3 hp are listed in 10 CFR 431.446 (DOE small electric motor standards). There is no federal efficiency standard for DC motors.
Note: Connected pump and compressed air inventories assume a three-phase AC motor, so single-phase and DC motors cannot be connected to them. A motor that is already connected has to be disconnected before it can be changed to single-phase or DC.
Note: Line frequency, power factor, efficiency class, synchronous speed, poles and VFD do not apply to DC motors and are hidden. Values entered before switching to DC are kept. The efficiency entered for a DC motor should include the field circuit and the drive or controller if the energy for those is part of what you want to track.
Full-Load Speed
The rotational speed of the motor at full load. This speed is the speed at which maximum torque can be achieved.

Full-Load Amps
The maximum current that the motor can draw/supply safely by meeting (either supplying or consuming) the full-load conditions.
"Estimate Full-Load Amps" will predict the full-load amps of the motor using standard electrical engineering equations. The equations use the following predictor variables:
  • Est. Nominal Efficiency
  • Full-Load Speed
  • Rated Motor Power
  • Line Frequency
  • Rated Voltage
Note: If the values used to predict the full-load amps are changed then the button will have to be re-clicked to update the prediction.
Note: This calculation is only valid for 3-phase AC motors
